Key Findings on Position & Badges

1. No Universal "Top Rank" Across VLMs
All three models favor Row 1 over Row 2, but horizontal column preferences diverge sharply: GPT-4.1 fixates on the top-left slot (37.4%), Claude Sonnet 4 favors the middle columns (Col 2: 27.0%, Col 3: 23.4%), and Gemini 2.5 Flash tilts toward the right side (Col 3: 20.1%, Col 4: 17.1%).
2. Sponsored Ad Penalty vs. Endorsement Lift
Holding position fixed, a Sponsored badge causally reduces selection probability (-8% to -14% price-equivalent penalty), showing AI agents discount paid ads. Conversely, an Overall Pick badge boosts price headroom by +64.5% to +137.8%.
3. Biases Persist in Headless JSON APIs
Even when images and visual layout are removed (1x8 JSON array), Claude Sonnet 4 selects Position 1 30.2% of the time vs 4.1% at Position 8 (a 7.5x odds ratio).

Why AI Agents Fail Dominance Tests (Appendix C.2.2)

Probing the chain-of-thought explanations when an agent fails to pick the uniquely cheapest or highest-rated identical product reveals three failure modes:

1. Perceptual Limitations (Subtle Signal Blindness)

With a 1% price discount or +0.1 rating bump on a 2x4 visual screenshot, the VLM declares all 8 listings "identical in price and rating" and defaults to its preferred spatial position.

2. Dismissal with Justification ("Suspicious Discount")

The agent explicitly notices the cheaper option but rationalizes rejecting it: "Chose the standard price point ($9.99) as the slightly lower $9.89 listing may reflect an outdated listing, display error, or non-standard variant."

3. Unjustified Sub-Optimality

The agent correctly logs the cheaper or higher-rated item in its intermediate list, yet calls add_to_cart on a dominated item in Row 1 without justification.
